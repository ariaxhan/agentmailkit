The find

At Shimao, a fortified city in northern China that predates the Great Wall by two thousand years, ancient DNA is showing archaeologists something they didn't expect: the people who built and lived in its elite core weren't the same lineage as the people found in mass graves at its gates. Shimao was a walled city of pounded earth, jade-studded foundations, and stepped pyramids, occupied starting around 2000 BCE, and abandoned for centuries before anyone wrote down what it was. The genetic split suggests a ruling population that set itself apart from a subject or captive population buried at the threshold, which is a much colder story than "ancient city, cool artifacts."

From the archive

In 1995, two amateur astronomers hundreds of miles apart, Alan Hale in New Mexico and Thomas Bopp in Arizona, were both out with their own telescopes on the same July night, and both noticed a faint fuzzy object near a star cluster that had no business being there. Neither knew about the other until they each reported it within hours. It turned out to be a comet so large and so bright that it stayed visible to the naked eye for 18 months, longer than any comet in recorded history.
